Combat, gear and monsters

How fighting works, from your skill bar to your enchants.

← Back to Brightwake · Wiki home

Fast side-scrolling fights on long platforms, with skills that grow louder and bigger as you level them.

How combat feels

  • Hits land with a short hit-stop, punchy knockback and popping damage numbers.
  • A short input buffer and a little ledge grace keep jumps and combos forgiving.
  • Training grounds are long platforms joined by ropes and ladders.
  • Each job's resource has its own meter, so you can read your state at a glance.
  • Some branches clear crowds best (Ninja, Sorcerer); others melt bosses (Assassin, Sharpshooter).

Skills and the skill bar

  • You earn skill points every level and spend them in the skills window (K).
  • Your bar holds 8 active skills, and passives are always on.
  • Each skill has 10 to 20 levels, and its effect looks visibly bigger as it levels up.
  • By level 50 you have 13 active skills to choose your 8 from, plus 6 passives.
  • Skills marked ★ come from your guild's class quests rather than skill points.

See every skill on the skill lists page.

Bosses

Bosses have clear, readable tells before their big attacks, and change their pattern across phases. Watch, dodge, then punish.

Monsters and the journal

Every area has its own monsters: glass and sea creatures around Vitrea, clockwork and sand beasts around Brasswind, seasonal forest creatures around Solstara, and twisted things near the Hush.

The monster journal (J) records each monster you meet: its art, lore, drops, where it lives and its Flicker card. Completing an area's page earns a reward. The journal is shared by every character on your account.

Gear

  • Every piece of gear has an item level. Dungeons and the raid ask for a minimum average item level.
  • Gear wears down with use and costs gold to repair.
  • Glamour lets you wear the look of one item over the stats of another. Looks never change stats.

Enchanting

  • Gear has enchant slots by rarity, from 1 on common items to 5 on legendary items.
  • An enchant always succeeds if your Enchanter level is high enough, and the window warns you before you try.
  • If an enchant fails, you only lose the materials. Enchanting never damages or destroys an item.
  • You can remove an enchant for a fee.
